# Concept

## Character Approach

One consistent character who appears in different jobs and workplaces depending on what the situation needs. The job is a container for the story, not a fixed identity.

Reference: viral multi-job character videos (same face, many uniforms) and character-driven educational formats like Chloe VS History (follow the person into the scenario; the subject arrives because that is where they are).

## Content Pattern

Each short video follows the same skeleton:

1. Character is in a recognizable work moment.
2. The task looks like it needs a heavier model (Sonnet high / Opus low).
3. Character sets up Haiku medium with a tight prompt and runs it.
4. Result is usable. Character reacts naturally.
5. One short observation, then back to the work.

No direct “let me teach you Haiku” framing. The camera stays with the work.

## Rollout

- First 20 posts: motion graphics / motion design (lower cost, test situations).
- Next phase: AI-generated character clips in the same situations once the format is proven.
- Character consistency (face, voice, reaction style) is the priority once clips begin.

## Why This Gets Views

- Instant situational contrast (character in a specific workplace doing a specific task)
- First-person / self-narrating framing so the viewer follows along
- Recognizable stakes (task looks heavier than it is)
- Repeatable structure with high variety of settings
- People follow the character, not individual videos
