# Rights and Risk — read before generating anything

Not legal advice. Have an entertainment/IP attorney review before any
monetized release. Rules and platform policies change; verify current ones.

## Why not real athletes (LeBron, Kobe, Curry, etc.)
- **Right of publicity.** Using a real person's likeness in something
  commercial-looking, or that is monetized, without their consent is
  actionable in many US states (e.g. California Civ. Code §3344). For
  deceased people, California's §3344.1 extends the right post-mortem,
  which covers Kobe Bryant's estate.
- **False endorsement / trademark** (Lanham Act). A Gatorade-style ad with a
  star in it implies that the star and the brand endorse it. Brand names,
  logos, and NBA/team marks add to the exposure.
- **Platform policy.** YouTube, TikTok and Meta require labeling of realistic
  AI content and can remove content or demonetize accounts for impersonation
  or deceptive synthetic media. Many AI video tools also block real-person
  likenesses.
- **Practical downside.** The more a video takes off, the more likely it is
  to get a takedown, a strike, or a demand letter — and the earnings from it
  can be clawed back. The success case is the risky case.
- Federal "digital replica" legislation has been proposed (NO FAKES Act);
  check its current status.

## What to do instead
- Original fictional characters; do not make thinly-veiled lookalikes of
  real players either.
- Fictional or properly licensed brands only. No Gatorade, NBA, or team marks.
- Original music/voice only.
- If you ever want a real athlete, get a written license from them or their
  representation (agent or estate); some athletes do license AI likenesses.
