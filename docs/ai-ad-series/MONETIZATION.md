# Monetization

Thresholds below were accurate to my knowledge but change often — verify on
each platform's current Creator/Partner page before planning around them.

## Direct platform payouts (slow, small to start)
- **YouTube Partner Program** — roughly 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000
  public watch hours or 10M Shorts views in 90 days for full ad revenue;
  a lower tier exists for fan funding. YouTube's policy against
  mass-produced/repetitive content applies; distinct, original episodes help.
- **TikTok Creator Rewards** — requires a follower and view threshold and
  original, longer videos.
- **Instagram/Facebook** — invite-based bonuses and ad revenue sharing vary.

## Where the real money usually is
1. **Sponsorships / brand integrations.** Once an episode gets traction, sell
   the sponsor tag card (local gyms, sports shops, drink brands) at a flat
   fee or CPM rate.
2. **Sell the service (best fit for Gray Matter).** Each episode is a
   portfolio piece. Offer "AI commercial production" to local businesses:
   a 30-sec spot package with a fixed price. This does not depend on virality.
3. **Affiliate links** in descriptions (gear, training tools, AI tools used).
4. **Own the characters.** Because they're original, we can later sell
   merch, a cartoon/short-form series, or license them — something we could
   never do with a real athlete's likeness.
5. **Newsletter / community** — behind-the-scenes "how it's made" posts,
   paid templates, or a prompt/shot-list pack.

## If a video takes off — checklist
- Pin a comment/link to a landing page (could live on this site).
- Capture emails; don't rely on one platform.
- Turn on monetization and reply to sponsor inquiries within 24 hours.
- Set up an LLC-level bank account and track income (QuickBooks is connected
  in this workspace); set aside money for taxes.
- Keep the source files and the generation log (proof of originality).
