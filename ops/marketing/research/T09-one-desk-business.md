## The problem in the owner's words

"Ever driven back to the shop just to open one file?" "Emailing yourself attachments so you can work from home?" "Nobody else can cover for you." These are the phrasings owners use when files and logins live on one computer.

## What is actually going on

CISA's Cyber Essentials guidance tells small business leaders to require multi-factor authentication for all users, beginning with privileged, administrative and remote access accounts. [1]
The same guidance says to track who is on your network, including user accounts, vendors and partners, and to set procedures for staff transfers and terminations. [1]
It also says to establish regular automated backups and redundancies of key systems. [1]
NIST's guide to telework and remote access says these technologies let staff, contractors and vendors work from outside locations. [2]
NIST says every component used for remote work, including BYOD devices, should be protected against threats identified through threat modeling. [2]

## Post angles

- A Sunday-night quote request that waits until Monday because the price sheet lives on the shop computer.
- Driving back to the office just to open one file that you need from a job site.
- Asking who else could open the business files tomorrow if you were out sick.
- A former employee's login still working months after they left, because nobody removed it.
- A pre-departure check: can you reach the schedule and invoices from your phone before you leave the job?
- Emailing yourself attachments as a workaround that stops being acceptable once the files hold client data.
