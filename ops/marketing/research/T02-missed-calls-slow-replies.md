## The problem in the owner's words

"I was under a car when they called, and by the time I called back they'd booked someone else." "The quote request sat in my inbox all weekend." "I answer messages at night, after the kids are down, and by then they've moved on."

The owner is busy doing the work and cannot reply in the moment. No verified figure for how many jobs are lost this way was found, so posts describe the moment and make no numeric claim. The main research source covers online sales leads, not phone calls.

## What is actually going on

A Harvard Business Review article by Oldroyd, McElheran, and Elkington, based on research about online sales leads, states in its abstract: "Our research shows that most companies are not responding nearly fast enough." [1]

The SBA's guidance on website contact pages is that visitors need a clear way to get in touch with the business, and the contact page should provide essential details [2].

## Post angles

- A quote request comes in Friday at 6 p.m., and the first reply goes out Monday morning.
- Two missed calls while you have the clippers in your hand, and no text goes back.
- A customer asks "are you open Saturday?" by DM and sits unanswered until the weekend is over.
- The same pricing question gets typed out three times in one week.
- The lead who messaged a competitor first is the one who booked.
- A detailer's voicemail is full at the end of the day, and nobody can leave a message.
- An estimate request sits in a form inbox nobody checks on Sunday.
