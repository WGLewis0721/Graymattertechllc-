## The problem in the owner's words
"The laptop slid off the seat and that was every customer I have."
"I think the backup runs. I've never actually opened it."
"My photos of finished jobs are only on the desktop in the back office."

## What is actually going on
CISA calls the 3-2-1 rule a trusted guideline for backups [1].
The 3-2-1 rule calls for 3 copies of important files, 2 different types of storage media, and 1 copy stored off-site, away from the business location [1].
CISA advises protecting backups with physical security, encryption and offline copies [1].
CISA says it is important to perform scheduled recovery tests to verify backup integrity [1].
CISA advises testing that your team can rapidly restore data both fully and partially [1].
CISA's small business guidance tells the IT lead to perform and test backups, schedule them, test restores and document a restoration plan [2].

## Post angles
- A laptop slides off the passenger seat on the way to a job, and every customer record is on it.
- A phone photo of a signed contract exists nowhere else, and the phone is the only device with it.
- The desktop fails the morning a big estimate is due.
- The owner is asked when the last backup finished and cannot say.
- The first time anyone tries to restore a file is the day the computer dies.
- A laptop is stolen from a truck with the business still signed in on it.
- A file is deleted by accident from the shared folder, and nobody can find an older version.

## What Gray Matter does about it
Backup and Recovery sets up automatic backup of important business files, including email, documents and customer records, off the device. It tests backups regularly to confirm they work, keeps a clear recovery plan, and protects against accidental deletion, device failure and ransomware. The service page describes the outcome as a dead laptop becoming an inconvenience instead of a disaster.
