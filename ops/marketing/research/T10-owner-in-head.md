## The problem in the owner's words

"If the honest answer is 'in my head' or 'ask Mike,' that's the thing to fix." "Training a new person takes me weeks every time." "I can't take a week off without the wheels coming off." These are the phrasings owners use when the work only exists in one person's memory.

## What is actually going on

The SBA's disaster-recovery guidance advises owners to identify and document critical business functions and processes. [1]
Ready.gov advises owners to organize a business continuity team and compile a written plan to manage a business disruption. [3]
PNC notes that in small operations everything can hinge on the knowledge and expertise of one or two employees. [2]
PNC says that losing that knowledge, through a leadership change or an absence, can slow decisions down. [2]
PNC recommends writing key processes down as clear steps and roles, and says cross-training and clear succession planning may help keep the business running. [2]

## Post angles

- A new hire calling the owner four times before the first job starts because the steps only live in the owner's head.
- Two employees doing the same job two different ways because nobody wrote down the right way.
- The one job only the owner knows how to do, and what happens to it during a week away.
- Asking which three processes would hurt the business most if they stopped for a day.
- A ten-minute exercise: write the steps for one repeated task in plain words.
- A written checklist that replaces a shadowing week for a new cleaner or stylist.

## What Gray Matter does about it

Process Documentation turns how the work is actually done into written steps people can follow without the owner. It covers written procedures for repeated work, checklists for jobs, process maps showing who does what, and runbooks for things that go wrong. The documents are kept in one searchable place, in tools the business already pays for, with one current version.
