## The problem in the owner's words
"The invoice looked exactly like the last one from the supplier, but the bank account was new."
"The email said it was urgent and needed to be paid before noon."
"I got a login alert from a place I have never been."

## What is actually going on
The FBI IC3 describes a case where a business is requested to wire funds for an invoice payment to an alternate, fraudulent account [1].
The FBI IC3 says this pattern is also called the Bogus Invoice Scheme or the Invoice Modification Scheme [1].
The FBI IC3 advises verifying changes in a vendor's payment location by adding an additional layer of two-factor authentication [1].
The FBI IC3 advises using previously known phone numbers, not the numbers provided in the email request, to verify such changes [1].
The FBI IC3 advises establishing other channels, such as telephone calls, to verify significant transactions [1].
CISA warns that phishing messages may use a false sense of urgency or importance to push you into acting quickly [2].
CISA advises that if you are unsure an email request is legitimate, you should verify it by contacting the company directly, without using contact details from the suspicious message [2].
CISA says an unsolicited email asking you to open an attachment is a common malware delivery method [2].
CISA advises turning on MFA across systems such as email, file storage and remote access [3].
CISA describes SMS and email codes as the form of MFA that provides the weakest protection [3].
CISA's small business guidance advises regularly checking that staff are using MFA for key systems, especially email [4].

## Post angles
- The invoice from a regular supplier shows a new bank account at the bottom, and the email looks exactly like the last one.
- A "quick" wire request arrives from the boss's email while the boss is on a job site with no signal.
- A customer calls to ask why a deposit went to an account with a different name on it.
- A login alert arrives early in the morning from a city the owner has never visited.
- An attachment called "updated_invoice" arrives from a contact who has never sent a PDF before.
- The bookkeeper gets a message that says the payment must go out before noon.
- An electrician's real invoice was copied, and the only thing changed is where the money goes.
