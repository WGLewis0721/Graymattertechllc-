## The problem in the owner's words
"There's a charge on the card every month and I can't tell you what it is."
"I signed up for a free trial in the spring. I'm still paying for it."
"We have two scheduling apps and nobody remembers which one is the real one."

## What is actually going on
The FTC advises checking your debit or credit card for charges after you cancel a subscription [1].
The FTC advises keeping a copy of your cancellation request [1].
The FTC says that if a company keeps billing you after you have cancelled, you can file a chargeback dispute with your card company [1].
The FTC advises saving letters, notes and emails about a subscription problem, because they could help prove you are entitled to a refund [1].
The SBA advises categorizing expenses on your balance sheet, including recurring and nonrecurring costs [2].
The SBA says that looking closely at money in and money out helps maintain a sustainable balance between profit and loss [2].
The SBA says you should keep tracking your costs once you put a plan into action [2].

## Post angles
- A Monday bank statement review turns up a charge for an app nobody has opened since spring.
- A free trial meant to end after one month is still charging the card in the next billing cycle.
- The renewal notice for a software license that belonged to a former employee arrives in the owner's inbox.
- Two booking tools are both paid for, and each team member uses a different one.
- The card expires, and the owner has to dig through old emails to find which subscriptions were still attached to it.
- A new hire asks what a charge is for, and nobody in the shop can answer.
- A renewal email for a website template arrives, and nobody remembers the last time the template was opened.
