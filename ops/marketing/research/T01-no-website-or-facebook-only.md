## The problem in the owner's words

"People keep asking me for my prices in DMs." "Someone asked if I have a website and I sent them my Facebook page." "They looked me up, couldn't find my hours, and went somewhere else."

The owner sees the cost as lost walk-ins and lost bookings, not as a technology problem. No verified figure for lost jobs was found in the sources, so posts describe the moment and make no numeric claim.

## What is actually going on

Google lets a business show its website on its Business Profile, but the link has to be entered as a full address that begins with http:// or https:// [1].

To appear in Search and Maps, a business must add or claim its Business Profile and then verify it [1].

Google says some business categories can add more links to their profile, such as online orders, reservations, or appointments [1].

The U.S. Small Business Administration says that if you want an online presence for your business, you should start by registering a domain name [2].

The SBA says a visitor has less than 10 seconds to understand what a business offers, citing Nielsen Norman Group research, so the homepage has to say what you do immediately [3].

The SBA lists the homepage, About Us, products or services, contact, and testimonials as essential pages for a small business website [3].

The SBA article is an older blog post, so its page list is a starting point, not a current standard [3].

## Post angles

- A customer searches your business name on a Tuesday night and finds a Facebook page with no hours and no prices.
- A client asks for your website while you have clippers in hand, and you send a Facebook link instead.
- The same pricing question arrives in DMs for the tenth time this week.
- Your Google listing has a phone number but no way to see services or book.
- A new homeowner searches "detailer near me" and calls the next business on the list.
- Your business card has an Instagram handle and no web address.
- Booking happens by DM after 9 p.m., when you are not answering.
