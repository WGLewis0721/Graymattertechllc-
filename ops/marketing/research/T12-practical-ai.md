## The problem in the owner's words

"I type the same reply to the same question every evening." "Somebody told me AI would do my proposals, but I don't trust what it says." "I'm not going to paste my client list into some website." These are the phrasings owners use when they want the time back but are wary of the risks.

## What is actually going on

The FTC says that using consumer data for other purposes without clear and conspicuous notice and affirmative express consent risks violating the law. [1]
In February 2024 the FTC said it may be unfair or deceptive for a company to adopt more permissive data practices, such as using data for AI training, through a surreptitious, retroactive change to its terms. [2]
The FTC also says a business that collected data under one set of privacy commitments cannot then unilaterally back away from them. [2]
NIST's AI Risk Management Framework includes a Generative AI Profile, which NIST says can help organizations identify unique risks posed by generative AI. [3]

## Post angles

- The same appointment-confirmation reply typed again every evening, and a saved draft that cuts that down.
- A first draft of a proposal written by a tool, then checked line by line against your own prices and dates.
- Before pasting a client's details into a tool, checking what the company does with what you type.
- A myth that a tool which says it is private is private.
- A weekly summary of the messages that came in, drafted for you to review.
- A rule for legal wording: the tool can draft it, but a qualified person signs off.

## What Gray Matter does about it

Our view, not a sourced fact: AI saves real time on drafting replies, summarizing long threads and producing first drafts of documents, such as a proposal outline or a staff handout. A person must check anything that involves prices, dates, legal wording or customer data before it goes out. Technology Partner offers ongoing support for questions, issues and small requests, plus a second opinion before a business commits to a new tool.
